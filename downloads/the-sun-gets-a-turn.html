The Sun Gets a Turn

A d100 for what the desert does on its own turn, and a d20 for why today is worse than usual. Ten bands, two rolls a travel day, no stat blocks.

The same tables, running. Both roll in the browser, the travel day is built as a two-box clock, and the heat clock is already built. Offline, no login. It also installs into Gnasher’s Grimoire.

Your players are four days into a hundred miles of dry country and the only thing that has happened to them is a giant scorpion. That is not a monster problem. For four days the most dangerous thing on the map has been sitting there doing nothing, and the desert has been a beige room with an encounter table bolted to the wall. This fixes it with one rule and two tables: the desert takes its turn on the day, so you roll a d100 twice per day of travel, once in the morning and once in the late afternoon. A hundred rows in ten bands, and almost none of them deal damage on purpose. They cost water, time, distance, gear or morale. Plus a d20 for why today is worse than usual, rolled once before they set out, and a card that is the whole thing on one printable page. No stat blocks, nothing to convert. Free to use at your table and in your own material under CC BY 4.0.

75. A cairn that has been rebuilt. Stacked stones, put there deliberately and kept that way. Cairns are built by people and rebuilt by people, and the stones on top of this one are a different colour from the stones at the bottom. Somebody comes out here. Recently.

100. Nothing. No wind. No weather. Nothing on the horizon and nothing underfoot. The desert skips its turn. After a week of it doing something twice a day, one turn of it doing nothing is the loudest row on this table.

d20, 20. Nothing is wrong. Good weather. Full skins. Easy footing. Every local they passed on the road this morning was going the other way.
