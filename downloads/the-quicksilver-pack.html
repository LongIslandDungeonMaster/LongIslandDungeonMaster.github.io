The Quicksilver Pack

They don't want to eat you. They want you to run.

Feywild hounds that hunt for sport, not survival. They don't need to kill fast, so they don't: the toying with prey is the whole point of the hunt, and prey that's still afraid is worth more to them than prey that's already dead.

They never open with teeth. First comes a voice: a friend calling for help, a dog barking somewhere it shouldn't be, your own voice answering from ground you haven't walked. By the time anyone hears a growl, the party is already split up and the hounds are already circling whoever went to check.

Field Notes

how to run a hunt that starts before the fight does
Gnasher's Take

The fight doesn't start when the fangs come out. It starts the moment your table hears a voice that shouldn't be there. Let them argue about whether to go check. That argument is the encounter, and if you run it right, your players split the party themselves and blame nobody but the woods.

How to run it

The plain-English version: a pack ambush where the trap is a sound, not a pit.

Flip for the five-beat breakdowntap to flip
  1. The setup. Someone hears something impossible before anyone sees a hound. Roll on What You Hear, or pick the sound that would pull one specific character away from the group.
  2. Seed the omens. One roll on Signs You're Being Hunted as the party investigates, escalating. Let them notice the pattern before you explain it.
  3. The split. If someone goes to check the sound alone, that's the whole design working. The nearest 2-3 hounds converge on them using Encircle; the rest keep the remaining party occupied with more false sounds so reinforcements don't arrive fast.
  4. The fight, when it comes, is quick and mobile. Bite, Encircle, and Blink-Step to flank or bail. No hound trades a losing exchange it doesn't have to.
  5. They quit the moment it stops being fun for them: a hound down, or the prey stops reacting with fear. The whole pack blinks out at once and the woods go dead quiet. That's worse for a table than watching them lose a fight, and they know it.

The win condition isn't just the kill. Staying together when a voice calls you away is the actual test; the fight is what happens to the party that fails it.

The Tables

roll the hunt
What You Hear (d8)
1A companion's voice, calling your name, from off in the dark, pain in it.
2A dog barking in the distance, then a yelp cut short.
3Someone singing, off-key, a song only one of you would know.
4Hoofbeats approaching fast, then nothing.
5A child crying, alone, close by.
6Your own voice, calling back to you from somewhere you haven't been.
7The clatter of a dropped weapon and a startled shout.
8Laughter, soft, right behind you, gone when you turn.
Signs You're Being Hunted (escalating) (d10)
1Every animal sound in the area stops at once.
2Silver hairs caught on bark, too fine to be wolf.
3A perfect paw print in soft earth, and no others leading to or from it.
4The temperature drops sharply for no reason.
5Something answers a question nobody asked out loud.
6Your own shadow moves a half-step out of time with you.
7A distant howl, then a second, closer, then a third, closer still, then silence.
8Mist gathers at ankle height, moving against the wind.
9You find one of your own footprints ahead of where you've walked.
10Something laughs, delighted, from very close, and there's nothing there.
Where the Hunt Finds You (d6)
1An old fae ring of standing stones, half-sunk in a bog.
2A logging road that shouldn't dead-end this deep in, but does.
3A hollow where the trees grow in a perfect, unnatural circle.
4The ruins of a hunting lodge, doors still swinging.
5A frozen-over pond that groans underfoot even in summer.
6A crossroads where the signpost points four directions to the same town.

Who it’s for. Run as a pack of 4-6, the Quicksilver Pack is a CR 1/2-per-hound encounter (five hounds = 500 XP raw, 1,000 XP adjusted): a hard-to-deadly ambush for four 3rd-level PCs, a solid medium for four 4th-level, and a warm-up by 5th. It opens with Voice-Throwing to split the party, never teeth first, and disengages the moment a hound drops or the prey stops flinching. If the ambush actually lands and the party splits up, run it a step hotter than the math says: a divided party is fighting a worse fight than the numbers alone suggest.

Encircle. The hound has advantage on its attack roll against a creature that has another Quicksilver Hound within 5 feet of it and able to fight.

Voice-Throwing. As an action, the hound perfectly mimics any sound or voice it has heard, seeming to come from any point it can see within 120 feet. A creature can tell the sound isn't real only by succeeding on a DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) check.

Actions

Bite. Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 7 (2d4 + 2) piercing damage.

Bonus Actions

Blink-Step (Recharge 5-6). The hound vanishes in a flicker of quicksilver light and reappears in an unoccupied space it can see within 30 feet.

Gnasher's Notes

A pack that hunts for sport is the best kind of trouble, because the fight doesn't start when the fangs come out. It starts the moment your table hears a voice that shouldn't be there. Let them argue about whether to go check. That argument is the encounter. Don't play the hounds as cruel, play them as delighted, the way a cat is delighted by a string, and your table will feel the wrongness faster than any growl could sell it. Split the party for real if they take the bait, because the whole design lives or dies on someone standing alone in the dark for one bad minute. Once steel comes out, they don't linger: whichever hound isolated a target gangs up with whoever's nearest, and any hound in a losing spot blinks clear rather than trade a hit it doesn't need to trade. The second the game stops being fun for them, whether that's one hound down or the party simply stops flinching, the whole pack blinks out at once and the woods go quiet. That's worse for a table than watching it lose, and the hounds know it. Stay hungry, Gnasher.

Ossa Vane, the one who came back alone

Role: a trapper who led four companions into the fae woods and returned by herself.
Wants: to warn everyone away, or, if desperate enough for coin, to guide the party in exchange for never having to go back alone.
Voice: flat, catalogues the tells like a checklist, won’t meet your eyes when asked what she saw.
The line: “They don’t want to eat you. They want you to run. Don’t run.”

Stay hungry. — Gnasher
