The King Under the Floor

Under the oldest inn in Millbrook sits a knot of rats grown together at the tails, and for thirty years its subjects have carried it tribute: every ring and key and coin and letter this town has ever lost.

Rat catchers know the thing the trade does not advertise. Sometimes rats knot. Tails fuse, a dozen bodies drag one shared swollen life around under the boards, and the loose rats feed it and serve it and bring it things. Nothing down there was ever destroyed, because that is not what tribute is for. The King barely moves. Everything interesting here is what the other three do around it, and all of it runs on rules you already know: movement costs, saving throws, and one short standing order per caste. Standing up, this court is a nuisance you could clear with a broom. Under two feet of joist it is a horror, bought with movement costs rather than a new mechanic. Four stat blocks, a d20 of what thirty years put under the floor, and one building with three ceiling heights. Free to use at your table and in your own material under CC BY 4.0.

13. A gold signet ring cut with a swan over a bar. This is Barnaby Bly's, and the parish register says Barnaby Bly drowned upriver thirty years ago, unwitnessed, wearing it. A ring cannot come off a drowned man forty miles downstream and end up under a floor in Millbrook, and everyone the party shows it to will work that out at about the same speed.

18. A locket snapped at the hinge, one pin missing. It is Hesta Dunmore's. Hesta Dunmore owns the mill, sits on the vestry, and reported this stolen the same week Barnaby Bly drowned. The missing hinge pin is in the midden too, about a foot away. It does not matter at all.

7. A man's linen cuff, torn at the seam. What has dried on it is not river mud. Nobody in Millbrook has worn linen at the cuff for thirty years, which narrows it a great deal more than the party will realise at the time.
