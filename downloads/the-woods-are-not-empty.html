The Woods Are Not Empty

Five small tables of what the forest is doing while the party walks through it. Animals, insects, small finds, foraging, and water.

The same five tables, running. Each one rolls in the browser, so the answer is one tap away without breaking the walk. Offline, no login. It also installs into Gnasher’s Grimoire.

A forest the party walks through is a corridor. A forest that is busy is a place, and the difference is one plain sentence about something that is happening whether or not anybody rolled for it. These are five flat tables you roll whichever way round you like: a d20 of what is in the trees, a d12 of insects, a d12 of small things you find, a d20 of foraging, and a d20 of water. Nothing here attacks. Nothing here is a quest. Nothing here needs a stat block, a DC, or a rule your table does not already have. Roll the one that answers the question in front of you, ignore the other four, and read the row out as a thing that is going on. Every row works in any temperate forest. Free to use at your table and in your own material under CC BY 4.0.

Trees, 6. Two bucks have locked antlers and one of them has been dead long enough that the other is dragging it.

Small finds, 10. Somebody's axe is sunk in a stump, rusted in, and the stump has grown up around the head.

Water, 13. The water is so clear the bottom looks a foot down and is four.
