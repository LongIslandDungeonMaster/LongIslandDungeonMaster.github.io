# The Night Watchman

### A solo adventure for one character. No Dungeon Master required.

**Night Rating: 2 of 5 lanterns.**
For one 3rd-level character. Two to two and a half hours. Set in Brackenford.

*From the Long Island Dungeon Master. Written by hand, by a human, over thirty years of running games for other people. Every word of it.*

---

## What this is

This is a night, not a dungeon.

You are the new night watchman of the village of Brackenford: one lantern, one route, five posts, and the hours between dusk and dawn. You answered a notice on the board because nobody else did. Tonight the ford freezes over, which happens perhaps three times a year and never used to happen at all, and by midnight you will be the only person in the village who is not behind a door.

There is no Dungeon Master. The book is the Dungeon Master. It will describe what you see, ask you what you do, tell you when to roll, and remember what you did. You supply the character, the dice, and the nerve.

You cannot win this adventure. There is nothing at the end of it that you defeat. Read that now rather than discovering it at four in the morning: this is a night you survive and a village you keep, and the book is honest with you about that from the first page. There are five endings and not one of them is the correct one.

## What you need

- **A 3rd-level D&D character.** Yours, built normally, any class, 2014 or 2024 rules. Or take a pregen from Appendix B and start in two minutes.
- **Dice.** The usual set.
- **One page for your night sheet.** Appendix D is printable if you want it, but the back of an envelope is traditional.

## Why 3rd level

Because a lone character at 1st level dies to a single lucky hit, and a book that kills you on page four has not told you a story, it has wasted your evening. At 3rd level your subclass is online, which means this book can notice what you actually are. It does, constantly. A Battle Master reads a frozen street differently from a cleric of the dawn, and the entries know it.

If your character is a different level, play them as they are and change nothing. The encounters are tuned for 3rd and the Watch Lamp below will absorb the rest.

## What you track

Four things. Nothing else. If you find yourself keeping a fifth list, the book has failed and you should stop doing it.

**Your character sheet.** Hit points, spell slots, the usual.

**Codewords.** Single words in small caps. When an entry grants one, write it down. Later entries look for them, sometimes hours later. The codewords are the night's memory, and they are how this book knows what kind of person you were at two in the morning. In the last stretch of the night the book may also hand you a **dawn mark**, which is a codeword the finale writes and only the dawn reads. Same box on the sheet. You will not need to remember it long.

**The Dawn Track.** Twelve boxes. Tick one whenever an entry says to. Investigating things costs night. Standing in the cold getting your breath back costs night. You are not being punished. You are being timed, the way a night times you. **If the twelfth box ticks before you have reached the Last Watch:** finish the scene you are in, then go to entry **315**. Dawn is coming, and the thing at the ford is done waiting. If it ticks during the Last Watch, keep playing: the dawn takes the finale as it finds it.

**Lamp oil.** Four units at Lantern Watch. When an entry says the lamp drinks a unit, mark one off; the cold makes it drink, and so do certain choices. You can never carry more than your starting number. When you mark off the last one, your lamp is dark: outdoors you have disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks, and some entries have a line that only happens to a dark lamp, and you do not want those lines. A dark lamp relights from any lit fire in the village, or a Rekindle token. Not from wishing.

## Choose your Watch Lamp

Three ways to play the same night. Pick one now. Change it at any rest, as often as you like, and the book will never mention it again.

**Ember Watch.** You want the night and not its teeth. You start each act with 10 + your level in temporary hit points, you carry two Rekindle tokens, every printed DC drops by 2, you get five oil, and you cannot die. At 0 hit points you are dragged to safety by dawn and the night goes on without you. In the dead hours you reach three of the four crises instead of two, so you will also see more of this village than anyone else.

**Lantern Watch.** The night as written. 5 + your level in temporary hit points at the start of each act, one Rekindle token, DCs as printed, four oil. You can lose. Every dying moment has a rescue that costs you something.

**Dark Watch.** The night does not care about you. No temporary hit points, no Rekindle, DCs as printed and worse late, three oil, and enemies win initiative ties. Death is death, and there is an ending written for it. Bury the watchman and make another one.

**Rekindle token.** Spend one to reroll any d20 you have just rolled, or to stabilise yourself at 0 hit points, or to refill one unit of lamp oil. One mechanic, three uses, two boxes on your sheet.

**Watch Lamp sidebars.** Combat and hazard entries end with a single line giving all three dials at once, always in the same order:

> **Watch Lamp** · **Ember:** the shrike has 14 HP and does not use Silent Rush. · **Lantern:** 22 HP. · **Dark:** 22 HP, and it tries to push you off the arch.

Read your own and ignore the other two. They change numbers, never story: no tier sees a different night, only a different amount of teeth.

## How to play, if you have never done this alone

You read an entry. It tells you what you see. At the bottom it offers you choices, or asks for a roll, and each option gives you a number. Go to that entry. That is the whole engine.

Three things worth knowing, because they are the three that trip people up:

**When the book asks for a check, it means it.** Both outcomes are written. Failure is never a wall in this adventure and never a restart; it is a different, usually worse, way forward. You are allowed to fail. Some of the best parts of this night are on the failure side.

**When the book is silent, you decide.** It cannot cover everything and it will not try. If you want to know what a room smells like or what a man's hands are doing and the book has not said, decide, and be generous, and err toward whatever makes the night more interesting. You will not break anything. There is no wrong answer that the book is secretly marking.

**You will want to fix it.** You will not be able to. Around midnight this adventure puts four emergencies in front of you and lets you reach two, and the fact that you had to choose is the point rather than a flaw. Choose, and live with it, and do not go back and re-read the ones you missed until you have finished the night. They will be there next time.

## A worked example

> ### 041
>
> You put your eye to the gap in the shutter.
>

>

>
> → Knock. **043**
> → Ring in and go. **047**

You roll a d10 plus your Wisdom modifier... no. You roll a **d20** and add your Wisdom modifier, because it is a Wisdom saving throw and this book will always tell you exactly which die and which number. You get 11. That is a failure, so you tick a box on the Dawn Track, and you have lost a little of the night to standing in a square getting your breath. Then you decide: knock, or walk away.

Nobody is watching. Take the whole evening.

## THE HANDOFF

---

### 001

The bell has not rung yet, but the light is going.

You stand at the ford with your boots in the mud and a borrowed tabard over your coat, and Hett Vance looks you over the way a man looks over a cart he is not sure will make the hill. He is a head taller than you and twenty years older and he has been the entire militia of Brackenford for longer than anyone says out loud.

"You came," he says. He sounds like he had a speech ready for if you didn't.

He holds out a lantern. It is heavy, brass, scratched to hell, and already lit.

→ Take it. **002**

---

### 002

"Four rules," Hett says, and counts them on his fingers like he has done it before, to men who are not here anymore.

"One. The lamp stays lit. Two. You ring in at the posts, so the village hears you're alive. Three." He stops. Starts again. "Three, if you see something you don't understand, you do not chase it. You come and get me."

You wait. He does not give you a fourth.

"Four's the one I can't teach," he says. "You'll know it when you need it."

He puts the bell-clapper in your other hand. Brass, cold, the size of a spoon.

→ Ask him something. **003**
→ Say nothing and start the circuit. **007**

### 003

*Choose one. You may come back here and ask again until you leave.*

→ "Why me?" **004**
→ "What happened to the other watchmen?" **005**
→ "What am I actually looking for?" **006**
→ That's enough. Start the circuit. **007**

### 004

"Because you read the notice," he says. "And because you're standing here."

He is quiet a moment. Somewhere up the slope a shutter bangs closed, then another, the sound moving through the village like something passing.

"That's not nothing. People in this village have got clever at looking at a thing and deciding it's somebody else's. You looked at it and came down the hill." He straightens the tabard on your shoulder, tugs it flat, an old gesture from a man who used to have a row of them to straighten. "So. You."

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 005

The answer takes long enough that you hear the river fill the gap.

"They went to Aldermere," Hett says. "Errand for me. Six of them." He is watching the far bank while he says it. "Long errand."

*If your passive Wisdom (Insight) is 13 or higher, or you make a DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check:* he is not lying, and he is not telling the truth either. He sent them. Whatever the errand was, he invented it, and he is glad every day that they went.

You do not know why yet. But you know it, and he knows you know, and he does not look at you.

Write the codeword **VOUCHED**.

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 006

"Nothing," he says. "That's the honest answer. Ninety nights in a hundred you walk the round, you get cold, you ring the bell, you go home."

He rubs his jaw.

"The other ten, you'll know, because the village goes wrong. Dogs stop. Smoke goes straight up when there's a wind. Water does what water shouldn't." He nods at the ford, at the brown water sliding over the flat stones. "That one especially. You watch the water."

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 007

Then the dusk bell rings.

It comes from the shrine at the edge of the square, one clean stroke and then another, unhurried, exactly on time as it has been every dusk for a month from behind a door nobody has opened. Hett stops with his mouth half open and waits it out. So does the whole village. You can feel it happen, a few hundred people going still at once and then going on.

When it finishes, Hett says, "Right," and it is the most relieved you have ever heard a man sound about a bell.

He walks away up the slope without looking back.

You are the watch.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Walk to the water's edge and look, as he said. **008**
→ Get on with the circuit. **009**

---

### 008

*Make a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check, with disadvantage if your lamp is not lit.*

**Success.** The brown water is sliding over the flat stones the way it always has, except at the edges, in the slack water behind the biggest rocks, where it has gone still and grey and slightly wrong, like a mirror breathed on. It is skinning over. In autumn. In an hour it will bear a coin. By midnight it will bear a man.

The ford is icing, and you are the only person in Brackenford who knows it yet.

Write the codeword **ICEBOUND**.

→ Go and find Doran. **010**

**Failure.** The water looks like water. Cold, brown, ordinary, loud over the stones. You will feel a little stupid about this later.

→ Get on with it. **009**

---

### 009

You turn your back on the river and start up the bank, and the cold comes with you.

Not wind. There is no wind. Just the temperature going down a step at a time, the way a house cools when someone opens a door in another room.

*If Nell is with you:* she takes up her place half a stride ahead, ears up, tail governing itself, entirely satisfied that the two of you are doing something normal. She has walked this village her whole life. She has never once done it in a tabard's company, and she approves.

Doran Welk is still in his chair at the near bank, which is unusual, because Doran goes in at dusk.

→ Go to him. **010**

---

### 010

He has not gone in. He is sitting in the chair he carried out here himself with the strongbox between his boots, and he is not collecting anything, because there is nobody to collect from. He is just sitting, facing the water, with his cudgel across his knees and his coat buttoned to the throat.

He hears the tabard before he sees it. Something in his shoulders drops.

"Oh," he says. "It's you. Vance found somebody."

He takes a copper out of his own pocket and tests it against his teeth. Then he does it again.

→ "Why are you still out here?" **011**
→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

---

### 011

"Waiting," he says.

"For what?"

He looks at the water. He is a broad, tired man and up close he is more tired than broad.

"You're new, so I'll say it plain and then I'll not say it again. There are nights in this village when it is better to be somewhere with a door." He tests the copper against his teeth. "I'm working out if this is one."

→ "How do you tell?" **013**
→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

---

### 012

He does not ask you if you are sure. That is the part you will remember.

He stands up so fast the chair goes over backward, and he picks up the strongbox, and for one moment, before he gets his face back, you see exactly how frightened a man can be in a village where people wave in the street.

"Then I'm to go up the hill," he says, mostly to himself. "Tonight. All right. All right."

"Up the hill where?"

"Do your round," Doran says. Not unkindly. He is already walking. "Ring in at your posts. Stay off the far bank. And if you see me on the hill road later, watchman, you did not."

Write the codeword **VOUCHED** if you do not have it.

→ Watch him go, then start the circuit. **020**

---

### 013

"You don't, till it's on you," Doran says. "That's the trick of it. That's why nobody's ever ready."

He tests the copper against his teeth.

"Cold comes down off the hill, quiet. Water goes strange. Then it's a freezing night, and the village pays what it owes, and in the morning we all say wasn't that a cold one and we go about our business." He puts the copper away. "You want a rule? Here's the only one worth the tabard. When it turns, get people behind doors. Not you. Them. You're the one outside. That's the job you took."

→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

## THE CIRCUIT

---

### 020

**The route.**

The watch runs a circle: up from the ford through the square, along the top of the village past the inn, down the back way by Greel's yard, and out to the broken bridge before it comes back to the water. Five posts with a bell-ring at each, and Hett's four rules, and about four hours of dark before midnight.

You will not get to all five. You never do. That is what the bell is for: the village hears where you are, and where you are not.

*Choose a post you have not yet visited. When you return here, choose again.*

→ **The Broken Wheel**, the inn at the top of the square. Light in the windows. **030**
→ **The Shrine of the Quiet Hand**, at the square's edge. The door that does not open. **040**
→ **Greel's Yard**, between the square and the river. Somebody is still working. **050**
→ **The Square and the Well**, the heart of it, the notice board. **060**
→ **The Broken Bridge**, and past it, across the water, the Drowned Lane. **070**

*After your second post, do not choose a third. Go to **090**.*
*After your third post, the round is walked. Go to **100**.*

## POST: THE BROKEN WHEEL

---

### 030

You hear it before you see it, which is the nicest thing that will happen to you tonight: forty people being warm at each other through a wall.

Inside, the common room is low and gold and full. Mara Tullamore is working the taps with her sleeves shoved up past the elbow, running an argument about sheep with three men at once and winning all three. Somebody is playing something badly. The fire is enormous.

You are standing in the door in a wet tabard with a lantern, and the room notices, one table at a time, and goes quieter than a room that size should be able to.

→ Ring the bell and go. **031**
→ Go in. **032**

---

### 031

You ring in from the step. The clapper on the brass plate by the door, twice, the way Hett showed you.

Inside, the noise comes back up like water closing over a stone. Somebody laughs. The badly played thing resumes.

You stand outside a warm room in the cold and understand the job you have taken better than you did an hour ago.

→ Back to the route. **020**

---

### 032

Mara sees the tabard and something crosses her face that is not quite a smile.

"Well," she says. "Hett got one."

She pours you something without asking and sets it on the bar, and while she does it, without breaking stride or conversation, she takes a clean mug from the rail, walks it to the empty chair by the fire, and sets it down in front of nobody.

Nobody at the tables reacts. They have watched her do it every dusk for a year.

She looks once at your lantern, reaches under the bar, and stands a small stoppered flask next to your drink. Lamp oil. "House rule," she says. "The watch carries spare. The last one didn't."

**Add one unit of lamp oil**, up to your Watch Lamp's starting number.

→ Ask about the chair. **033**
→ Ask what the village is worried about tonight. **035**
→ Drink it, thank her, go. **036**

---

### 033

"That's Tobin's," she says. "My husband. He'll be along."

She says it in exactly the voice she used for the sheep argument. Not brave. Not broken. Just a woman stating the arrangements.

The three men at the bar find things to look at.

*If you press her, or make a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check:* she puts both hands flat on the bar.

"He went out on the moor last autumn and he did not come back, and I know what everyone in this room thinks that means. But on the night the ford froze, I heard him at my door. I opened it." Her voice does not change at all. "There was nothing there but his crook, stuck upright in the frost, and frost does not hold a crook up, watchman. Something stood it there."

Write the codeword **MUGSET**.

→ Ask what the village is worried about tonight. **035**
→ Let it be. **036**

---

### 035

"Tonight?" She wipes the bar. "Tonight they're worried it's cold."

She lets that sit for a second.

"You'll hear a lot of daft things in here and about one in ten of them is worth carrying. So carry this one. When it gets cold like this, quick, no wind, the sort where your teeth hurt? Get the old ones and the little ones behind a door and let the rest of us call you a fool in the morning." She takes your empty cup. "Nobody in Brackenford has ever once been embarrassed about a door."

*If you have **ICEBOUND**:* you tell her the ford is skinning over. Mara stops moving. Then she turns to the room and calls last orders, two hours early, in a voice that does not invite discussion, and forty people look at her, and start putting their coats on. Nobody argues. That frightens you more than the water did.

→ Go. **036**

---

### 036

Outside, the cold has got worse while you were warm, which is the cost of being warm.

You ring in at the plate by the door. The sound goes out over the roofs and comes back thin.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## POST: THE SHRINE OF THE QUIET HAND

---

### 040

The shrine is the oldest building on the square and it looks it: small, stone, squat, with a carved hand above the door, palm out, fingers together. *Stop. Wait. Be still.*

The door has not opened in a month. There is a drift of dead leaves against it that has been rained on and frozen and rained on again until it is less a drift than a seal.

There is candlelight through the shutter gap.

An hour ago that bell rang, on time, from in there.

→ Look through the gap. **041**
→ Knock. **043**
→ Ring in and leave. Rule three. **047**

### 041

You put your eye to the gap in the shutter.

Candles. Dozens, most of them guttered to stubs and left where they died, on the floor, on the sill, in a rough line down the middle of the nave that does not correspond to anything. At the far end, past the altar, a shape sits upright in a chair facing the door with its hands on its knees.

It is a man. He is awake. His eyes are open and they are pointed at the door, and the door is where you are.

*Make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, you step back from the shutter fast enough to scrape your lantern on the stone, and you take no damage, but tick one box on the Dawn Track: you stand in the cold getting your breath for longer than you would admit.*

→ Knock. **043**
→ Ring in and go. **047**

### 043

You knock.

It is a small sound to make on a square that size.

Nothing. Then, from the other side of the door, close, at the height of a seated man, a voice like a page turning.

"The door stays shut."

→ "I'm the watch." **044**
→ "Are you all right in there?" **045**
→ Leave him to it. **047**

### 044

A long pause.

"Vance found someone." Something moves behind the wood. "Good. That is good. Listen to me, watchman, because I will not open this door and you have somewhere to be."

"I'm listening."

"The bell rings at dusk and it rings at dawn and it has not missed in a month. That is the whole of it. The bell is a lock on this village, and the ringing is the key turning in it, twice a day, on time. As long as it turns, something stays out." The voice is patient in a way that is worse than raving. "Do not ask me what stays out, because I do not know. Ask me instead what you will do if one evening you are out on your round and you hear nothing."

You do not have an answer.

"Then get one," says Brother Anselm, "before you need it."

Write the codeword **HANDSIGN**.

→ "What happens if you stop?" **046**
→ Go. **047**

### 045

"No," the voice says, without any self-pity at all. "I am not. Thank you for asking. Nobody has asked."

A dry sound that might once have been a laugh.

"I have not slept in thirty days. I am not certain any longer which of the things I have seen were in the room. I am certain of the bell, because the bell is brass and it is heavy and my hands know the weight of it, and a man can hold on to a weight when he cannot hold on to anything else."

Write the codeword **HANDSIGN**.

→ "What happens if you stop?" **046**
→ Go. **047**

---

### 046

The silence behind the door goes on long enough that you wonder if he has died sitting up.

"I do not know," Anselm says at last. "That is my whole sin, watchman. I have built a month of my life and possibly this entire village on a thing I cannot test, because the only way to test it is to stop." A shift of cloth. "So I do not stop. And every dusk I ring it, and every dusk nothing terrible happens, and every dusk that proves precisely nothing, and I do it again."

Then, quieter, and this is the part you will still be hearing at midnight:

"If it does not ring, do not come here. Go and get the children."

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Go. **047**

From inside the shut door, faintly, a man's voice says amen.

## POST: GREEL'S YARD

---

### 050

Greel's yard is a fenced acre of everything: pelts, iron, rope, dried fish, a wall of mismatched boots gone stiff in the cold. Pell Greel is not here. Pell Greel is at his supper, being warm.

Bryn is here. The boy is twelve or so and he is stacking crates by lantern light with his coat sleeves pulled over his hands, working alone in a yard the size of a barn, an hour after he should have been let go.

The goat is eating a hat.

### 051

"He knows." Bryn does not stop stacking. "There's a load."

"At this hour?"

"There's a load," the boy says again, in the exact flat tone of somebody repeating the last thing an adult said to him.

→ Help him stack. **052**
→ Ask about the load. **053**

### 052

You set the lantern on a barrel and take the other end of the crates.

It is honest work and it goes fast with two, and after a while Bryn stops being careful around you, the way children do when an adult has proved they will actually lift something. He tells you the goat's name. He tells you which boots on the wall are the good ones. He tells you Pell Greel has never once written a number down and has never once been wrong, and that he is not sure this is a good thing.

Then he says, without any change in his voice, "You're the watch, so you'll be out at midnight."

"I will."

"Then don't be by the water."

→ "Why not?" **053**

---

### 053

Bryn puts down the crate.

He is a quick, watchful boy who goes everywhere in this village and is invisible doing it, and right now he is neither quick nor invisible. He is a child in a cold yard trying to say something.

His mouth opens. Nothing comes out. He tries again, and you can see him get as far as the shape of the first word before it goes.

*If you have a lit lamp and you crouch to his level, or you make a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) or Wisdom (Insight) check:* **054**
*Otherwise, or if you would rather not push a frightened child:* **055**

---

### 054

You crouch, so the lantern is between you and low, and you are not a tall shape in a tabard anymore.

"You don't have to say it," you tell him. "Point."

Bryn thinks about that for a long moment. Then he lifts his arm and points, straight and steady, past the fence, past the roofs, up the black slope of the hill behind the village to where Caer Brack sits against the last of the light.

Then he points down. At the ground. At *under*.

"I went down," he says, and that comes out fine, all of it, every word, because it is not the thing itself. "On a dare. Down the swept stair. And I came back up." He is shaking, and not from the cold. "I've tried to tell. Four times. It won't come."

→ **055**

→ Ring in and go. **056**

## POST: THE SQUARE AND THE WELL

---

### 060

The square is empty, which at this hour is right, and lit, which is not.

Somebody has left a lantern burning on the lip of the dry well in the middle of the square. Not a candle in a window. A good brass lantern, full of oil, set deliberately on the stones, casting a wide clean circle of light around the wellhead and nothing else.

The notice board stands at the edge of the light with its layers of paper going grey.

→ Go to the well and the lantern. **061**
→ Read the notice board first. **063**
→ Ring in and go. Something about the light is wrong. **066**

---

### 061

The lantern is warm. It has been lit maybe an hour, which puts it at the dusk bell.

The well it stands on has been dry for years. There is a plank cover, warped, pushed aside by about a hand's width, and the gap is the kind of black that a lantern a foot away does not touch at all.

*Make a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check.*

**Success.** The plank was pushed aside from *underneath*. The scrape marks on the stone lip are on the far side of the gap and they are fresh, pale against the weathered rock. Whatever moved this cover was in the well.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.** → **062**

**Failure.** It is an open well and a left lantern and you are cold and new to this. → **062**

---

### 062

You do not go down the well.

Write that down somewhere and be proud of it. Rule three. Hett Vance would shake your hand.

You do slide the plank back over the gap with your boot, which takes both hands and a shoulder, and it is heavier than it looks, and the whole time you are doing it you are extremely aware of the width of the space it is covering.

"How long have we got?"

"Before it's back?" Hett looks up at the hill, at the square black shape of Caer Brack against a clean cold morning sky. "It said within the month. It's not lied yet, as far as anyone can tell. That's the thing about it that nobody's ready for. It doesn't lie. It just waits until you've stopped being able to bear it and then it's reasonable at you."

He shrugs.

"Sefa'll say a fortnight. Alder'll say we've time. Anselm's got his doors open, which he hasn't in a month, so make of that what you like." He starts walking again. "So: not long. Are you in?"

→ Take it. **524**
→ Say no. **526**

### 526

You say no, and Hett Vance takes it exactly the way you would want a man to take it, which is to say completely, immediately, and without a single word of argument.

"Right," he says. "Then thank you. I'll not pretend I'm not sorry."

You give him back the lantern at the bottom of the hill.

You are two streets away when you hear him, out of sight, announcing the hour to an empty street the way he has done for a year, and you stop walking, and you stand there in the road for a long moment.

The book is not going to tell you what you did next. That one is yours.

→ **530**

### 530

**Here ends the night watch.**

You were the watch for one night, on the night the ford froze, and you did not beat anything. There is nothing under the hill that is any weaker this morning than it was yesterday. The swept stair is still swept. The candle at the bottom of it is still burning. Eleven is still eleven, or ten, or however the ledger stands now, and somewhere out on the moor an old woman is counting stones and getting the wrong answer.

But the village woke up.

That is the whole of it, and it is not nothing, and Hett Vance was right about the size of the job and wrong about what it is worth.

**THE END.**

*You can play this night again. Take a different route at dusk, keep different company in the dead hours, and stand somewhere else when it comes across the ice. There are four other endings and none of them is the correct one.*

*If you would rather keep going, turn to **Appendix A: After the Dawn**, and keep playing Brackenford freehand. The village has two weeks of quiet at most before the cold tries again, and now you know what walks it.*

### 535

**Dark Watch only.**

You do not come back at dawn.

They find you on the ice at first light with the frost already in the seams of your coat and packed white in the folds of your ears, and there is not a mark on you anywhere, and the ground under you is not thawed at all.

Hett Vance carries you up the slope himself and will not let anybody take the other end.

They bury you on the third day, next to Doran Welk, and the whole village comes. Goodwife Alder speaks. She is gracious and warm and says almost nothing, and she does not catch anybody's eye.

The ledger is settled. Brackenford will have a quiet winter, and the ford will run all season, and in the spring the children will paddle in it.

You were the watch for one night. Eleven was always going to be somebody.

**THE END.**

*Bury the watchman properly, then make another one and go back to **001**. The night runs differently for a different person, and Brackenford is going to need somebody on the round again in about two weeks.*

## Appendix A: After the Dawn

*A small oracle, so the night does not have to be the end of it.*

Brackenford has two weeks of quiet at most before the cold tries again. If you want to keep playing it without a Dungeon Master and without another book, you need exactly two things: a way to ask questions, and a way to be surprised. Here they are on one page.

### Asking the night a question

Frame anything as a yes or no question and roll d6.

| d6 | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | **No, and** something worse. Add a complication you did not expect. |
| 2 | **No.** |
| 3 | **No, but** something small goes your way. |
| 4 | **Yes, but** it costs you. |
| 5 | **Yes.** |
| 6 | **Yes, and** more than you asked for. |

If the answer seems very likely, roll twice and take the higher. Very unlikely, roll twice and take the lower. That is the entire system and it is enough.

### Being surprised

Whenever you roll a 1 or a 6 on the question above, or any time the night has gone quiet and you do not know what happens next, roll d8:

| d8 | The night offers |
|---|---|
| 1 | Somebody who should be inside is outside. |
| 2 | A thing that was shut is open. Nobody will admit to opening it. |
| 3 | An offering has been taken, and it was not the weather. |
| 4 | Somebody tells you the truth, at a bad moment, for their own reasons. |
| 5 | The cold arrives early, and everything you had planned is now happening in it. |
| 6 | A person you have written off turns out to have been doing something all along. |
| 7 | Somebody asks you for a promise you should not make. |
| 8 | It is quiet. Genuinely quiet. Take the win and use the hour. |

### Where to point it

The Brackenford Starter Setting has eight keyed locations, eight named villagers, a notice board table, and four simmering tensions, and this night has just moved every one of them. Roll the notice board. Ask who is collecting the toll now that Doran is dead. Ask what Goodwife Alder wants over tea. Ask what Sefa's count comes to this week.

The Wilderness Pack's tables cover the moor and the road, if you go out to the stones.

Do not go down the swept stair yet. That one has its own book coming.

## Appendix C: what the watchman knows

*A page for the player who wants to walk in cold. Everything here is common knowledge in Brackenford. None of it is a spoiler.*

**The village.** A few hundred souls at a river crossing on the edge of wild country. The road comes in from the south and crosses the River Wend at the ford, because the bridge lost its far arch a generation ago and was never repaired. Up the far slope: the square with the well and the notice board, the Shrine of the Quiet Hand at its edge, Greel's yard running down toward the river, and the Broken Wheel at the top with the lights on. Above everything, on the hill, the ruined keep of Caer Brack, which nobody goes into and everybody can see.

**The far bank.** Across the ford, the Drowned Lane: one row of half-sunk houses, abandoned a generation ago, nobody crosses to it after dark.

**The people you might meet tonight.** Hett Vance, the militia captain with no militia, who hired you. Doran Welk, who collects a copper at the ford and takes the blame for it. Mara Tullamore at the Broken Wheel, whose husband Tobin walked onto the moor last autumn and did not come back. Brother Anselm, who has not opened the shrine in a month and rings the dusk bell anyway. Pell Greel, who trades everything and sells information to both sides. Bryn, who runs Greel's goods and is twelve and sees everything. Goodwife Alder, who has no title and runs the village from her kitchen. Old Sefa out on the moor, who tends the stone circle and counts under her breath.

**The freezing nights.** Perhaps three times a year now, where it used to be once, the cold comes down off the hill with no wind behind it and the ford ices over. People who are outside on those nights sometimes do not come back. The village handles this by going indoors and not discussing it.

**Your job.** Walk the round. Ring in at the posts so the village hears you are alive. Keep the lamp lit. And if you see something you do not understand, do not chase it: go and get Hett.

You will break that last one. Everyone does.

## A word from the Long Island Dungeon Master

Thirty years behind a screen teaches you one thing above everything else: the table does not remember your plot. It remembers the moment somebody had to choose, out loud, in front of their friends, and could not have both.

This adventure is built out of that. There is no puzzle in it that a clever player unlocks. There is a night, and four things happening at once, and one of you. The whole design of it, from the Dawn Track to the two visits in the dead hours, exists to put you in front of a choice that costs something and then to remember, quietly, in a codeword, what you decided.

If you have never played alone before and this was your way in: welcome. There is a whole shelf of us out here.

If you have soloed for years and came to see whether a Dungeon Master could actually write one of these: I hope the prose earned its keep. In a solo book the writing is the Dungeon Master, and I have never once believed that a random table can do that job on its own.

Either way, tell me how the night went. I read all of it.

**The Long Island Dungeon Master**
