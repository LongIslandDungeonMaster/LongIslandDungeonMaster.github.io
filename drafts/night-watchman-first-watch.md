# The Night Watchman
## Act One: First Watch (dusk to midnight)

**Draft v0.1** for red-team review. Solo adventure for one 3rd-level character, no DM required.
Setting: Brackenford. Spec: `specs/the-night-watchman-spec.md`.

> **Draft notes for the red team.** Character level revised from 1st to 3rd (rationale in the spec changelog). Entry budget for this act came in at 64 authored entries against a planned 35, because the act uses a three-of-five post structure: a single run plays roughly 36 of them. That is deliberate replay content, not overrun, but it does mean the whole-adventure budget in the spec needs revising upward or the later acts need trimming. Entry numbering is sparse on purpose, with gaps inside each post for expansion; 034, 037 to 039, 042, 048, 049, 057 to 059, 067 to 069, and 079 to 089 are reserved, not missing. Codewords granted in this act: eight. Dawn Track consumed: three to five steps of twelve.

---

## Before you begin

You need: a 3rd-level character (yours, or a pregen from Appendix B), dice, and one page for your night sheet. On the night sheet you track four things and nothing else:

**Codewords.** Single words in small caps you write down when the book grants one. Later entries look for them. They are the night's memory.

**The Dawn Track.** Twelve boxes. Tick one whenever an entry tells you to. When the twelfth is ticked, dawn comes, ready or not.

**Lamp oil.** Four units at Lantern Watch. Some entries burn one. Without a lit lamp you have disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks made outdoors, and some entries close to you entirely.

**Your character sheet.** HP, spell slots, the usual.

**Choose your Watch Lamp now.** Ember Watch if you want the night's story and not its teeth. Lantern Watch for the night as written. Dark Watch if you want it to be able to kill you. You may change at any rest. Nobody is keeping score.

**The contract.** When the book is silent, you decide. Err toward whatever makes the night more interesting. You will not break anything.

Turn to **001**.

## THE HANDOFF

---

### 001

The bell has not rung yet, but the light is going.

You stand at the ford with your boots in the mud and a borrowed tabard over your coat, and Hett Vance looks you over the way a man looks over a cart he is not sure will make the hill. He is a head taller than you and twenty years older and he has been the entire militia of Brackenford for longer than anyone says out loud.

"You came," he says. He sounds like he had a speech ready for if you didn't.

He holds out a lantern. It is heavy, brass, scratched to hell, and already lit.

→ Take it. **002**

---

### 002

"Four rules," Hett says, and counts them on his fingers like he has done it before, to men who are not here anymore.

"One. The lamp stays lit. Two. You ring in at the posts, so the village hears you're alive. Three." He stops. Starts again. "Three, if you see something you don't understand, you do not chase it. You come and get me."

You wait. He does not give you a fourth.

"Four's the one I can't teach," he says. "You'll know it when you need it."

He puts the bell-clapper in your other hand. Brass, cold, the size of a spoon.

→ Ask him something. **003**
→ Say nothing and start the circuit. **007**

---

### 003

*Choose one. You may come back here and ask again until you leave.*

→ "Why me?" **004**
→ "What happened to the other watchmen?" **005**
→ "What am I actually looking for?" **006**
→ That's enough. Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 004

"Because you read the notice," he says. "And because you're standing here."

He is quiet a moment. Somewhere up the slope a shutter bangs closed, then another, the sound moving through the village like something passing.

"That's not nothing. People in this village have got clever at looking at a thing and deciding it's somebody else's. You looked at it and came down the hill." He straightens the tabard on your shoulder, tugs it flat, an old gesture from a man who used to have a row of them to straighten. "So. You."

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 005

The answer takes long enough that you hear the river fill the gap.

"They went to Aldermere," Hett says. "Errand for me. Six of them." He is watching the far bank while he says it. "Long errand."

*If your passive Wisdom (Insight) is 13 or higher, or you make a DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check:* he is not lying, and he is not telling the truth either. He sent them. Whatever the errand was, he invented it, and he is glad every day that they went.

You do not know why yet. But you know it, and he knows you know, and he does not look at you.

Write the codeword **VOUCHED**.

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 006

"Nothing," he says. "That's the honest answer. Ninety nights in a hundred you walk the round, you get cold, you ring the bell, you go home."

He rubs his jaw.

"The other ten, you'll know, because the village goes wrong. Dogs stop. Smoke goes straight up when there's a wind. Water does what water shouldn't." He nods at the ford, at the brown water sliding over the flat stones. "That one especially. You watch the water."

→ Ask something else. **003**
→ Start the circuit. **007**

---

### 007

Then the dusk bell rings.

It comes from the shrine at the edge of the square, one clean stroke and then another, unhurried, exactly on time as it has been every dusk for a month from behind a door nobody has opened. Hett stops with his mouth half open and waits it out. So does the whole village. You can feel it happen, a few hundred people going still at once and then going on.

When it finishes, Hett says, "Right," and it is the most relieved you have ever heard a man sound about a bell.

He walks away up the slope without looking back.

You are the watch.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Walk to the water's edge and look, as he said. **008**
→ Get on with the circuit. **009**

---

### 008

*Make a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check, with disadvantage if your lamp is not lit.*

**Success.** The brown water is sliding over the flat stones the way it always has, except at the edges, in the slack water behind the biggest rocks, where it has gone still and grey and slightly wrong, like a mirror breathed on. It is skinning over. In autumn. In an hour it will bear a coin. By midnight it will bear a man.

The ford is icing, and you are the only person in Brackenford who knows it yet.

Write the codeword **ICEBOUND**.

→ Go and find Doran. **010**

**Failure.** The water looks like water. Cold, brown, ordinary, loud over the stones. You will feel a little stupid about this later.

→ Get on with it. **009**

---

### 009

You turn your back on the river and start up the bank, and the cold comes with you.

Not wind. There is no wind. Just the temperature going down a step at a time, the way a house cools when someone opens a door in another room.

*If Nell is with you:* she takes up her place half a stride ahead, ears up, tail governing itself, entirely satisfied that the two of you are doing something normal. She has walked this village her whole life. She has never once done it in a tabard's company, and she approves.

Doran Welk is still in his chair at the near bank, which is unusual, because Doran goes in at dusk.

→ Go to him. **010**

---

### 010

He has not gone in. He is sitting in the chair he carried out here himself with the strongbox between his boots, and he is not collecting anything, because there is nobody to collect from. He is just sitting, facing the water, with his cudgel across his knees and his coat buttoned to the throat.

He hears the tabard before he sees it. Something in his shoulders drops.

"Oh," he says. "It's you. Vance found somebody."

He takes a copper out of his own pocket and tests it against his teeth. Then he does it again.

→ "Why are you still out here?" **011**
→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

---

### 011

"Waiting," he says.

"For what?"

He looks at the water. He is a broad, tired man and up close he is more tired than broad.

"You're new, so I'll say it plain and then I'll not say it again. There are nights in this village when it is better to be somewhere with a door." He tests the copper against his teeth. "I'm working out if this is one."

→ "How do you tell?" **013**
→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

---

### 012

He does not ask you if you are sure. That is the part you will remember.

He stands up so fast the chair goes over backward, and he picks up the strongbox, and for one moment, before he gets his face back, you see exactly how frightened a man can be in a village where people wave in the street.

"Then I'm to go up the hill," he says, mostly to himself. "Tonight. All right. All right."

"Up the hill where?"

"Do your round," Doran says. Not unkindly. He is already walking. "Ring in at your posts. Stay off the far bank. And if you see me on the hill road later, watchman, you did not."

Write the codeword **VOUCHED** if you do not have it.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Watch him go, then start the circuit. **020**

---

### 013

"You don't, till it's on you," Doran says. "That's the trick of it. That's why nobody's ever ready."

He tests the copper against his teeth.

"Cold comes down off the hill, quiet. Water goes strange. Then it's a freezing night, and the village pays what it owes, and in the morning we all say wasn't that a cold one and we go about our business." He puts the copper away. "You want a rule? Here's the only one worth the tabard. When it turns, get people behind doors. Not you. Them. You're the one outside. That's the job you took."

→ "The ford is freezing." *(requires ICEBOUND)* **012**
→ Leave him to it. **020**

## THE CIRCUIT

---

### 020

**The route.**

The watch runs a circle: up from the ford through the square, along the top of the village past the inn, down the back way by Greel's yard, and out to the broken bridge before it comes back to the water. Five posts with a bell-ring at each, and Hett's four rules, and about four hours of dark before midnight.

You will not get to all five. You never do. That is what the bell is for: the village hears where you are, and where you are not.

*Choose a post you have not yet visited. When you return here, choose again.*

→ **The Broken Wheel**, the inn at the top of the square. Light in the windows. **030**
→ **The Shrine of the Quiet Hand**, at the square's edge. The door that does not open. **040**
→ **Greel's Yard**, between the square and the river. Somebody is still working. **050**
→ **The Square and the Well**, the heart of it, the notice board. **060**
→ **The Broken Bridge**, and past it, across the water, the Drowned Lane. **070**

*After your second post, do not choose a third. Go to **090**.*
*After your third post, the round is walked. Go to **100**.*

## POST: THE BROKEN WHEEL

---

### 030

You hear it before you see it, which is the nicest thing that will happen to you tonight: forty people being warm at each other through a wall.

Inside, the common room is low and gold and full. Mara Tullamore is working the taps with her sleeves shoved up past the elbow, running an argument about sheep with three men at once and winning all three. Somebody is playing something badly. The fire is enormous.

You are standing in the door in a wet tabard with a lantern, and the room notices, one table at a time, and goes quieter than a room that size should be able to.

→ Ring the bell and go. **031**
→ Go in. **032**

---

### 031

You ring in from the step. The clapper on the brass plate by the door, twice, the way Hett showed you.

Inside, the noise comes back up like water closing over a stone. Somebody laughs. The badly played thing resumes.

You stand outside a warm room in the cold and understand the job you have taken better than you did an hour ago.

→ Back to the route. **020**

---

### 032

Mara sees the tabard and something crosses her face that is not quite a smile.

"Well," she says. "Hett got one."

She pours you something without asking and sets it on the bar, and while she does it, without breaking stride or conversation, she takes a clean mug from the rail, walks it to the empty chair by the fire, and sets it down in front of nobody.

Nobody at the tables reacts. They have watched her do it every dusk for a year.

She looks once at your lantern, reaches under the bar, and stands a small stoppered flask next to your drink. Lamp oil. "House rule," she says. "The watch carries spare. The last one didn't."

**Add one unit of lamp oil**, up to your Watch Lamp's starting number.

→ Ask about the chair. **033**
→ Ask what the village is worried about tonight. **035**
→ Drink it, thank her, go. **036**

---

### 033

"That's Tobin's," she says. "My husband. He'll be along."

She says it in exactly the voice she used for the sheep argument. Not brave. Not broken. Just a woman stating the arrangements.

The three men at the bar find things to look at.

*If you press her, or make a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check:* she puts both hands flat on the bar.

"He went out on the moor last autumn and he did not come back, and I know what everyone in this room thinks that means. But on the night the ford froze, I heard him at my door. I opened it." Her voice does not change at all. "There was nothing there but his crook, stuck upright in the frost, and frost does not hold a crook up, watchman. Something stood it there."

Write the codeword **MUGSET**.

→ Ask what the village is worried about tonight. **035**
→ Let it be. **036**

---

### 035

"Tonight?" She wipes the bar. "Tonight they're worried it's cold."

She lets that sit for a second.

"You'll hear a lot of daft things in here and about one in ten of them is worth carrying. So carry this one. When it gets cold like this, quick, no wind, the sort where your teeth hurt? Get the old ones and the little ones behind a door and let the rest of us call you a fool in the morning." She takes your empty cup. "Nobody in Brackenford has ever once been embarrassed about a door."

*If you have **ICEBOUND**:* you tell her the ford is skinning over. Mara stops moving. Then she turns to the room and calls last orders, two hours early, in a voice that does not invite discussion, and forty people look at her, and start putting their coats on. Nobody argues. That frightens you more than the water did.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Go. **036**

---

### 036

Outside, the cold has got worse while you were warm, which is the cost of being warm.

You ring in at the plate by the door. The sound goes out over the roofs and comes back thin.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## POST: THE SHRINE OF THE QUIET HAND

---

### 040

The shrine is the oldest building on the square and it looks it: small, stone, squat, with a carved hand above the door, palm out, fingers together. *Stop. Wait. Be still.*

The door has not opened in a month. There is a drift of dead leaves against it that has been rained on and frozen and rained on again until it is less a drift than a seal.

There is candlelight through the shutter gap.

An hour ago that bell rang, on time, from in there.

→ Look through the gap. **041**
→ Knock. **043**
→ Ring in and leave. Rule three. **047**

---

### 041

You put your eye to the gap in the shutter.

Candles. Dozens, most of them guttered to stubs and left where they died, on the floor, on the sill, in a rough line down the middle of the nave that does not correspond to anything. At the far end, past the altar, a shape sits upright in a chair facing the door with its hands on its knees.

It is a man. He is awake. His eyes are open and they are pointed at the door, and the door is where you are.

*Make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, you step back from the shutter fast enough to scrape your lantern on the stone, and you take no damage, but tick one box on the Dawn Track: you stand in the cold getting your breath for longer than you would admit.*

→ Knock. **043**
→ Ring in and go. **047**

---

### 043

You knock.

It is a small sound to make on a square that size.

Nothing. Then, from the other side of the door, close, at the height of a seated man, a voice like a page turning.

"The door stays shut."

→ "I'm the watch." **044**
→ "Are you all right in there?" **045**
→ Leave him to it. **047**

---

### 044

A long pause.

"Vance found someone." Something moves behind the wood. "Good. That is good. Listen to me, watchman, because I will not open this door and you have somewhere to be."

"I'm listening."

"The bell rings at dusk and it rings at dawn and it has not missed. That is the whole of it. That is the lock." The voice is patient in a way that is worse than raving. "Do not ask me what it locks. Ask me instead what you will do if one evening you are out on your round and you hear nothing."

You do not have an answer.

"Then get one," says Brother Anselm, "before you need it."

Write the codeword **HANDSIGN**.

→ "What happens if you stop?" **046**
→ Go. **047**

---

### 045

"No," the voice says, without any self-pity at all. "I am not. Thank you for asking. Nobody has asked."

A dry sound that might once have been a laugh.

"I have not slept in thirty days. I am not certain any longer which of the things I have seen were in the room. I am certain of the bell, because the bell is brass and it is heavy and my hands know the weight of it, and a man can hold on to a weight when he cannot hold on to anything else."

Write the codeword **HANDSIGN**.

→ "What happens if you stop?" **046**
→ Go. **047**

---

### 046

The silence behind the door goes on long enough that you wonder if he has died sitting up.

"I do not know," Anselm says at last. "That is my whole sin, watchman. I have built a month of my life and possibly this entire village on a thing I cannot test, because the only way to test it is to stop." A shift of cloth. "So I do not stop. And every dusk I ring it, and every dusk nothing terrible happens, and every dusk that proves precisely nothing, and I do it again."

Then, quieter, and this is the part you will still be hearing at midnight:

"If it does not ring, do not come here. Go and get the children."

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Go. **047**

---

### 047

You ring in at the post outside the shrine. The clapper on the plate, twice.

From inside the shut door, faintly, a man's voice says amen.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## POST: GREEL'S YARD

---

### 050

Greel's yard is a fenced acre of everything: pelts, iron, rope, dried fish, a wall of mismatched boots gone stiff in the cold. Pell Greel is not here. Pell Greel is at his supper, being warm.

Bryn is here. The boy is twelve or so and he is stacking crates by lantern light with his coat sleeves pulled over his hands, working alone in a yard the size of a barn, an hour after he should have been let go.

The goat is eating a hat.

→ "Does Greel know you're still here?" **051**
→ Help him stack. **052**
→ Ring in and go. **056**

---

### 051

"He knows." Bryn does not stop stacking. "There's a load."

"At this hour?"

"There's a load," the boy says again, in the exact flat tone of somebody repeating the last thing an adult said to him.

→ Help him stack. **052**
→ Ask about the load. **053**

---

### 052

You set the lantern on a barrel and take the other end of the crates.

It is honest work and it goes fast with two, and after a while Bryn stops being careful around you, the way children do when an adult has proved they will actually lift something. He tells you the goat's name. He tells you which boots on the wall are the good ones. He tells you Pell Greel has never once written a number down and has never once been wrong, and that he is not sure this is a good thing.

Then he says, without any change in his voice, "You're the watch, so you'll be out at midnight."

"I will."

"Then don't be by the water."

→ "Why not?" **053**

---

### 053

Bryn puts down the crate.

He is a quick, watchful boy who goes everywhere in this village and is invisible doing it, and right now he is neither quick nor invisible. He is a child in a cold yard trying to say something.

His mouth opens. Nothing comes out. He tries again, and you can see him get as far as the shape of the first word before it goes.

*If you have a lit lamp and you crouch to his level, or you make a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) or Wisdom (Insight) check:* **054**
*Otherwise, or if you would rather not push a frightened child:* **055**

---

### 054

You crouch, so the lantern is between you and low, and you are not a tall shape in a tabard anymore.

"You don't have to say it," you tell him. "Point."

Bryn thinks about that for a long moment. Then he lifts his arm and points, straight and steady, past the fence, past the roofs, up the black slope of the hill behind the village to where Caer Brack sits against the last of the light.

Then he points down. At the ground. At *under*.

"I went down," he says, and that comes out fine, all of it, every word, because it is not the thing itself. "On a dare. Down the swept stair. And I came back up." He is shaking, and not from the cold. "I've tried to tell. Four times. It won't come."

Write the codewords **WITNESS** and **BLACKNAIL**.

→ **055**

---

### 055

You send him home.

He goes without arguing, which tells you something, and at the gate of the yard he turns around and says, "The load goes up the hill road. That's all the loads. That's all of them, since before I started." And then he runs.

The goat has finished the hat.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Ring in and go. **056**

---

### 056

You ring in at the yard post. Two strokes, brass on brass, and the sound goes out flat across the crates and dies against the pelts.

The cold has teeth in it now.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## POST: THE SQUARE AND THE WELL

---

### 060

The square is empty, which at this hour is right, and lit, which is not.

Somebody has left a lantern burning on the lip of the dry well in the middle of the square. Not a candle in a window. A good brass lantern, full of oil, set deliberately on the stones, casting a wide clean circle of light around the wellhead and nothing else.

The notice board stands at the edge of the light with its layers of paper going grey.

→ Go to the well and the lantern. **061**
→ Read the notice board first. **063**
→ Ring in and go. Something about the light is wrong. **066**

---

### 061

The lantern is warm. It has been lit maybe an hour, which puts it at the dusk bell.

The well it stands on has been dry for years. There is a plank cover, warped, pushed aside by about a hand's width, and the gap is the kind of black that a lantern a foot away does not touch at all.

*Make a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check.*

**Success.** The plank was pushed aside from *underneath*. The scrape marks on the stone lip are on the far side of the gap and they are fresh, pale against the weathered rock. Whatever moved this cover was in the well.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.** → **062**

**Failure.** It is an open well and a left lantern and you are cold and new to this. → **062**

---

### 062

You do not go down the well.

Write that down somewhere and be proud of it. Rule three. Hett Vance would shake your hand.

You do slide the plank back over the gap with your boot, which takes both hands and a shoulder, and it is heavier than it looks, and the whole time you are doing it you are extremely aware of the width of the space it is covering.

Then you leave the lantern burning, because whoever set it there set it there on purpose, and it is not your light to take.

→ The notice board. **063**
→ Ring in and go. **066**

---

### 063

The board is a slab of weathered oak layered a finger thick with the village's business. A lottery winner from three seasons back. A lost dog, the ink run with rain. Hett's standing offer for the watch, the one you answered, with your own thumbprint still on the corner of it.

And pinned over all of it, on a black-headed nail, a sheet that was not there this morning.

The hand is careful. Nobody signed it.

→ Read it. **064**

---

### 064

> *The bell rings at dusk. Do not be on the far bank when it stops.*

That is all of it.

The nail is black and square-headed and driven through four other notices to reach the wood.

*If you have **HANDSIGN**:* it is not Anselm's hand. You heard the man an hour ago through a door and you would know his careful liturgical cadence anywhere, and this is not it. This is somebody older, and in a hurry, and writing to be understood by whoever can read.

*If you have **WITNESS**:* Bryn told you the loads go up the hill road, all of them, always. Somebody in this village has been managing this for a long time, and somebody else has been leaving warnings about it on a public board where anyone might look up.

Write the codeword **BLACKNAIL** if you do not have it.

→ Take it down. **065**
→ Leave it up. Someone meant it to be read. **066**

---

### 065

You work the black nail out of the oak with your thumbnail and it comes hard.

The paper goes inside your coat, against your chest, and you will be aware of it there for the rest of the night like a hand on your sternum.

Behind you, out past the roofs and down the slope, the river has stopped making the sound it makes over the flat stones.

You did not hear it stop. You only notice that it has.

→ Ring in and go. **066**

---

### 066

You ring in at the well post, in the middle of the empty square, standing in a circle of somebody else's light.

The two strokes go out across the square and up the fronts of the houses. Nobody comes to a window.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## POST: THE BROKEN BRIDGE

---

### 070

The bridge is a ruin and has been since before your grandfather. The near arch still stands, solid enough to walk out on, and then it simply stops, forty feet up, over the water, with the far arch a broken stub on the other bank and a generation of air in between.

Nobody repaired it. The ford is ten yards downstream and free, except for a copper, except for tonight.

You walk out to the end of the standing arch because the post is out there, and because you want to see the far bank, and those are two different reasons and only one of them is the job.

→ Look at the far bank. **071**
→ Ring in and get off this bridge. **078**

---

### 071

The Drowned Lane sits on the far side of the water: one row of houses half sunk in the bog, doors swollen shut, roofs caved, reeds growing up through the floors. It was part of Brackenford once, before the far arch came down the year the trouble started. It has been empty for a generation.

There is a light in one of the windows.

Not a fire. Not a lantern like the one on the well. A low, steady, cold-coloured glow, in the third house along, behind a window that has no glass in it.

*Make a DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check.*

**Success.** → **072**
**Failure.** You watch it for a long moment and it does not change and you cannot make anything more of it. → **073**

---

### 072

The house at the dead end of the lane, the last one, furthest from the water, has a door.

That is what is wrong with it. Every other door down there has rotted off its hinges or swollen shut in its frame decades ago. That one has a door, and the door has boards across it, and the boards are pale.

New timber. Nailed from the outside. Somebody crossed the water to do that, and did it recently, and did it in daylight, because nobody in this village would be over there in the dark.

Write the codeword **LARDER**.

→ **073**

---

### 073

Below you and to the left, on the near bank where the reeds come down to the water, something has been at work.

At first you take it for fishing stakes. It is a line of broken reed-stems and old fence-posts standing up out of the mud, and there is something small on the top of each one.

You lower the lantern.

Voles. Six of them, and a rat, and a bird, each one pushed down onto a point and left there, neatly, at even spacing, like a shelf in a larder. The nearest is not old. In this cold nothing rots, so you cannot tell how long, but the frost has not taken it yet, and everything else out here is white.

Write the codeword **LARDER** if you do not have it.

→ Get off the bridge. **078**
→ Hold still and listen. **074**

---

### 074

You hold still.

The river is not making its sound. That is the thing your body worked out ten minutes ago and your head is only now catching up to. The Wend runs wide and shallow and loud over flat stones and it has run loud under this bridge every hour of your life, and it is not doing it now. It is going quiet under a skin of grey.

And in the new quiet you can hear, from the reeds below, something breathing that is not you.

It is not a call. Fen-shrikes make no call. That is how they live in a place where everything listens.

*If you have a lit lamp, you may raise it and look. Go to **075**.*
*If you would rather walk away right now, you may. Go to **078**, and take the codeword **LARDER** if you do not have it.*

---

### 075

You raise the lantern.

It is standing in the reeds twenty feet below the arch: a moor bird the size of a wolfhound, grey as standing water, long-legged, its head level with your knees if it were up here, which in about two seconds it will be. The beak is not a beak. It is a spike with a bird behind it.

It has been watching you for some time.

Then the wings go, and there is no cry, just the enormous soft rush of them, and it is inside your reach.

→ **076**

### 076

**FEN-SHRIKE**

Medium beast. AC 13. Speed 30 ft., fly 50 ft.
STR 12 (+1) DEX 16 (+3) CON 13 (+1) INT 3 (-4) WIS 14 (+2) CHA 5 (-3)

**Beak.** Melee attack, +5 to hit, reach 5 ft. Hit: 1d8+3 piercing damage.
**Silent Rush.** The shrike has advantage on its first attack roll against a creature that has not yet acted in combat.

**Tactics script.**
Round 1: the shrike attacks with Silent Rush, then does not stay. It flies twenty feet off and lands on the broken arch, out of reach, and looks at you.
Round 2 onward: it dives, attacks, and withdraws thirty feet on the same turn. It will not stand and trade. It is trying to make you step backward, off a bridge, in the dark.
If reduced to half its hit points or fewer: it breaks and flies for the far bank. It does not come back tonight.
If you retreat toward the village: it follows for exactly one round, then returns to the larder. It is not hunting you. It is hunting *here*, and you are standing in the pantry.

*If Nell is with you:* she plants herself at the break of the arch and gives the dark over the water everything she has. While she stands with you, the shrike's dives lose Silent Rush. Nothing gets to be silent near Nell.

> **Watch Lamp** · **Ember:** 14 HP, and it does not use Silent Rush; you hear the wings in time. · **Lantern:** 22 HP. · **Dark:** 22 HP, and if you are standing on the arch when it attacks, you must also make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or be pushed 5 feet toward the drop. Fall from the arch: 3d6 bludgeoning and you are in the freezing water.

*If you win:* → **077**
*If you flee to the near bank:* → **078**, and take the codeword **LARDER** if you do not have it.

### 077

It goes down in the reeds and does not get up, and the quiet comes back in over the top of it.

You stand on a broken bridge over a river that has stopped talking, with your heart going, and you understand for the first time that the lamp in your hand is not for seeing. It is so that the village can see *you*: a small moving light on the round, at the hours, where it should be.

Write the codeword **BLOODED**.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ Get off the bridge. **078**

### 078

You ring in at the bridge post.

Two strokes. And out across the water, from the empty houses of the Drowned Lane where nobody has lived in a generation, something rings twice back.

→ Back to the route. **020**

## THE COLD COMES IN

*Play this entry when you leave your second post. Do not choose a third yet.*

---

### 090

You are between posts when the night changes, and it does not do it gradually.

The cold arrives like a room. One step and your breath is a plume and your teeth ache in their sockets and the water on the cobbles has gone from wet to white. Behind you, up in the village, a dog that has been barking on and off all evening stops in the middle of it.

The smoke from the chimneys along the top of the street is going straight up. There is a wind. You can feel it on your face. The smoke is going straight up anyway.

Dogs stop. Smoke goes straight up when there's a wind. Water does what water shouldn't.

Hett gave you the list and you did not know it was a list.

Your lamp flame shrinks to a bead, steadies, and burns on, drinking hard against the cold. **The lamp drinks a unit: mark off one oil.**

*If Nell is with you:* she has stopped. She is pressed against your shin, not trembling, not growling, not making any sound at all, and that is the wrongest thing yet, because until this moment she has had an opinion about everything.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ **091**

---

### 091

Down the slope, at the bottom of the street where it turns toward the ford, something crosses the road.

It goes left to right through the gap between two houses, at the edge of what your lantern reaches, and it is on two legs, and it is the size of a man, and it is not walking like one. It is walking like someone doing an impression of walking who has only had it described to them.

It does not look up at your light. Everything in this village looks at a light.

It is gone in the space of a breath.

→ Go after it. Rule three says no. **092**
→ Get to a door and warn somebody. **094**
→ Stand absolutely still and let it go. **093**

---

### 092

You do not chase it. You get to the gap between the houses in time to see the alley behind them, and it is empty, and there is nothing in it but frost.

There are prints in the frost.

They are bare feet. They go from the middle of the alley to the wall of the house at the end, and they stop, and the wall is a wall.

*Make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw.* **On a failure, you spend a long moment with your back against the opposite wall and your lantern held out in front of you like it is a weapon, and you tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

Write the codeword **BLACKNAIL** if you do not have it: whoever wrote *do not be on the far bank when it stops* was not being poetic.

→ **095**

---

### 093

You stand still with your lamp low and you let it go, and it goes.

That is not cowardice. That is the fourth rule, the one Hett could not teach you, and it turns out the fourth rule is *know which of the two of you the night is actually about.*

→ **095**

---

### 094

You hammer on the nearest door.

It opens on a chain, and a woman's face appears in the gap, and she takes in the tabard and the lantern and your expression in about half a second.

You have a speech. You do not get to use any of it. She says, "Right," and shuts the door, and you hear the bar go across, and then you hear her moving through the house calling two names.

Nobody in Brackenford has ever once been embarrassed about a door.

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

→ **095**

---

### 095

The village is going dark around you as you stand there. Not sleeping. Closing. A shutter, then another, then a whole street of them, the sound moving away from you up the slope like something passing.

In four hours of this you have learned the shape of tonight: the water is stopping, the cold came down off the hill, a boy cannot say what he saw, a priest is holding a door shut with a bell, and a broad tired man has gone up the hill road with a strongbox to pay somebody.

You have one post left on the round and then it is midnight.

→ Take your last post. **020**

## MIDNIGHT

*Play this entry after your third post.*

---

### 100

You come back down to the water at the end of the round because that is where the round ends.

The ford is gone. Where the Wend ran wide and shallow and brown over the flat stones there is a grey floor, seamless from bank to bank, and the reeds at the edges are set in it like flies in amber. It is not the thin skin you saw at dusk. You could walk a horse over it.

Doran's chair is where it fell over. Doran is not in it.

→ **101**

---

### 101

*Count your codewords. This is the shape of your first watch, and the night will remember it.*

*If you have **ICEBOUND**:* you knew at dusk. You had four hours of knowing, and you spent them ringing a bell at posts, because that was the job you were given. You will think about that.

*If you have **VOUCHED**:* Doran Welk went up the hill road tonight with a strongbox and asked you not to have seen him, and you have not seen him come down.

*If you have **MUGSET**:* there is a clean mug sitting in front of an empty chair in the warmest room in Brackenford, and a woman who has heard her husband at the door on a night exactly like this one.

*If you have **HANDSIGN**:* a man who has not slept in thirty days told you to get one before you needed it, and you still do not have one.

*If you have **WITNESS**:* a twelve-year-old boy pointed up the hill, and then down, and could not make the words come.

*If you have **BLACKNAIL**:* there is a sheet of paper against your chest that says *do not be on the far bank when it stops.*

*If you have **LARDER**:* something has a pantry on the near bank of this river, and a door has been nailed shut from the outside at the dead end of the Drowned Lane.

*If you have **BLOODED**:* your hands have stopped shaking. Mostly.

→ **102**

---

### 102

Up on the slope behind you, a light is moving.

It is coming down the hill road, and it is coming fast, faster than a careful man goes on a bad path in the dark, and it is swinging the way a lantern swings when somebody is running with it.

You are the watch. You are the only person outside a door in this entire village.

→ Go to meet it. **103**

---

### 103

It is Hett Vance, and he has come down that hill road at a dead run in the dark, and when he sees your lantern he stops so hard he nearly goes over.

For a second, before he gets his face back, he looks at you exactly the way Doran looked at the water.

"You're still out," he says.

"You said ring in at the posts."

"I did." He is trying to get his breath and failing. There is frost in his beard. "Listen to me. I want you to go home. Not the round. Home, and bar it, and I will square it with anyone who asks and no one will."

→ "What's up the hill?" **104**
→ "Where's Doran?" **105**
→ "No." **106**

---

### 104

"Nothing you can help with," Hett says. "Nothing I can either, and I have had twenty years to work that out."

He looks past you at the ford, at the grey floor where a river used to be, and you watch him do the arithmetic of it, and you watch him arrive somewhere he has been before.

"That's the third one this year," he says quietly. "It used to be one."

→ **107**

---

### 105

Hett does not answer that.

He looks at the overturned chair on the bank, and the strongbox that is not beside it, and then at the hill road he has just come down at a run, and you understand that he knows exactly where Doran is, and that he has known for a year, and that this is the thing he sent six men to Aldermere to not be here for.

"Go home," he says. "Please."

→ **107**

---

### 106

"No," you say.

You are as surprised as he is.

Hett Vance looks at you for a long moment, a square-jawed man in a tabard he has no right to and a rank nobody granted him, who drills an empty yard and announces the hour to an empty street, and who has spent a year being the only thing standing between this village and the hill.

Something in his face gives out.

"All right," he says. "All right. Then you'll want to know the fourth rule."

→ **107**

---

### 107

Behind you, from the shrine at the edge of the square, the bell begins to ring.

It is midnight. It is not dusk and it is not dawn. In a month of a locked door and a careful, exhausted man keeping a schedule to the minute, the bell has rung twice a day and never once between.

It is ringing now. It is ringing fast, and badly, and much too hard, the way a bell rings when the man on the rope has stopped counting strokes and is simply hauling.

Hett Vance says, "Oh, no."

**Tick one box on the Dawn Track.**

**END OF FIRST WATCH.**

→ Continue to **Act Two: Middle Watch**, entry **200**.

## Appendix: night sheet quick reference

**Codewords available in First Watch:** ICEBOUND, VOUCHED, MUGSET, HANDSIGN, WITNESS, BLACKNAIL, LARDER, BLOODED.

**Dawn Track:** a full circuit of First Watch ticks three to five boxes of twelve. A player who investigates everything will reach Middle Watch with roughly half the night already spent. This is intended and it is the cost of thoroughness.

**Watch Lamp reminders.** Ember: 10+level temporary hit points at the start of each act, two Rekindle tokens, DCs as printed minus 2, five oil. Lantern: 5+level temporary hit points, one Rekindle, DCs as printed, four oil. Dark: no temporary hit points, no Rekindle, DCs as printed, three oil, and enemies win initiative ties.
