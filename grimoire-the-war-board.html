The War Board, step by step

Your own map images, a grid that actually lines up, fog you paint with a brush, light that behaves like light, and one press that puts it all on your players' phones. This page walks every control on the board, roughly in the order you meet them.

Open it, and point it at your maps

The board runs on the pictures you already own.

  1. Press Everything on the bar and choose War Board.
  2. Press Maps folder... and pick the folder of map images on your computer.
  3. Search the tray by name and press a map to open it on the board.
  4. Press Tokens folder... and do the same for your token pictures.
  5. Press + Scene to save what is on the board right now, fog and tokens and all, and Copy to make a second version of it.

Honest limitsNothing here is uploaded, because there is no account and no server to upload it to. The board needs no internet at all: the only part of it that reaches anybody else is the push, and that goes to the phones in the room.

The grid

Four ways in, because maps come out of the world crooked.

  1. Press Grid to open the aligning bar.
  2. Match: drag a box across a few of the map's own printed squares and the grid matches what it finds.
  3. Drop: type a square size in pixels and the grid drops straight on.
  4. Turn: give it an angle in degrees for a map that was scanned at a slant.
  5. Gridless for a picture with no squares in it at all, and Undo for the attempt you did not like.

Honest limitsA turned grid is newer than some copies of the Satchel. A player on an older one is flagged on your roster as grid not turned, rather than silently drawing their fog in the wrong place all night.

Fog, and what they can see

A brush, a rectangle, and two controls for the whole map.

  1. Reveal paints the squares the party can see, with a brush.
  2. Room reveals a rectangle in one drag.
  3. Hide puts squares back into the dark.
  4. All and None do the whole map at once, which is how most nights start.

Honest limitsFog belongs to the map and it is remembered, so a corridor you opened last week is still open. Your players and the second screen see what you revealed, not what you can see.

Tokens

Who is on the map, how big they are, who may move them, and what they can see.

  1. Choose Tokens to select and move them.
  2. Give one a label, a colour, and a size of one, two or three squares, or a free size in between.
  3. Set an owner: dm, or the name of a seat at your table, and that player may move their own.
  4. Give it sight in feet, and Darkvision when it has it. Normal, Dv 60 and Dv 120 are one press each.
  5. Player's art puts the picture from that player's own sheet on their token.
  6. Make a token... builds one out of any picture: drop a file, drag one out of another browser tab, or paste one, then crop it round and put a border on it.

Honest limitsA picture from a site that will not let a browser copy it can be shown but never saved, and the app tells you so instead of failing quietly. Drag the picture in from its own tab and that always works.

The ruler, the shapes and the ping

Four tools, and only one of them is private on purpose.

  • Measure drags a ruler across the map in squares and feet. Escape clears it, and it is yours alone: nothing is sent.
  • Draw gives you a pen, a line, a box, a ring and a written note, in eight colours and three widths. DM only keeps a drawing on your screen; otherwise it reaches your players live once the map is pushed.
  • Players lets a chosen seat draw as well, and Rub out clears what they drew.
  • Area sets a size in feet, then aims a circle, a cone, a line or a cube from a press on the map or on a token. Shared templates reach your players; DM only keeps them here.
  • Ping says look here: press the map and everybody at the table sees the ring.

Honest limitsThe ruler is the one tool on this list that is never shared, and that is deliberate. It is for you working out whether the blast reaches, not for telling the table that it does.

Light and walls

A wall stops sight. A torch carries its own bright and dim range. The two together are most of a dungeon.

  1. Press Lighting to turn it on, and set the ambient light: Dark, Moon, or brighter.
  2. Drag a wall to block sight, and use Gap to put a doorway in one.
  3. Press the map with Light to drop a light, or pick a preset: a candle, a lamp, a torch, a hooded lantern, a bullseye lantern, or the light of a spell, each with its own bright and dim range in feet.
  4. Type your own bright and dim numbers, pick the colour of the flame, and press On token so the light travels with whoever carries it.
  5. Undo wall, Remove light and Clear all take it back.

Honest limitsA creature's eyes are set on its own token, not on the map, so darkvision is a property of the thing looking. That is why two tokens standing in the same dark room can honestly see different amounts of it.

Pins, and the campaign map

The same board, holding a region instead of a room.

  1. Press Battle map and it becomes a Campaign map. Press it again to switch back: nothing is lost either way.
  2. A campaign map holds pins instead of tokens. Choose what a pin opens: a note, a page, another map, or a piece of text.
  3. Tap the map to place one, and drag a pin to move it. The search box finds the note, page or map you want it to point at.
  4. Party here drops the party marker, and Clear marker takes it off again.
  5. Set the scale in the bar: so many pixels to so many miles, kilometres, feet or metres, and the ruler reads in that.

Honest limitsThe party marker lives on a campaign map. Ask for it on a battle map and the app tells you to switch the map first, rather than putting a marker somewhere it would mean nothing.

Day and night, and the look of the map

A tint over your picture, driven by the hour in your world, and never a change to the file.

  1. Set the light on this map from the box on the bar: Light: the clock, or hold it at Dawn, Daylight, Dusk, Night, Sepia, Storm or Underdark.
  2. Left on the clock it follows the hour in your world: dawn from five in the morning, daylight from seven, dusk from six in the evening, night from eight.
  3. Tick To the table to show the light of day to your players as well as to you.
  4. Untick Day and night to turn the whole thing off on every map.

The hour it follows is the one on your calendar, and dragging that clock past dusk darkens the pushed map on every seat. The calendar, and the clock walks that end of it.

Honest limitsNone of this touches your map image. It is a tint over the picture and a look held on top, so the file in your folder is exactly the file in your folder, and the look you chose survives a reload.

The three starter maps

Plain tiles, for testing and a quick fight, so you are never stuck for a map.

  1. Open the maps tray and scroll to Starter maps.
  2. There are three: Parchment grid, Dungeon stone and Grass field.
  3. Press one and it opens with the grid already lined up.

The Grimoire draws these itself rather than carrying pictures of them, which is why they cost the download almost nothing. They are bare tiles: no props and no trees, so put your own on top.

Honest limitsThey are for testing and a quick fight, and the tray says so at the top of that section. The parchment and the stone are twenty squares by fourteen; the grass is twenty four by sixteen.

A map that moves

The animated map loops you already own, running under everything the board already draws.

  1. Put an mp4 or a webm in the same maps folder your still maps live in. There is nothing else to set up and nothing to convert.
  2. Open the maps tray and press it. A small film mark beside the name is how you tell a moving map from a still one.
  3. It plays under your grid, your fog, your lights, your tokens, your pins, your shapes and anything you draw, exactly where a still map sits.
  4. Press Pause on the board's toolbar to stop it on the frame you want, and drag the scrub beside it to run it forwards or back.
  5. Everything else works as it always did: Grid aligns on the moving art, Reveal and Hide paint fog over it, walls and lights sit on top of it, and Draw leaves your ink where you put it.

Pausing and scrubbing move the picture and nothing else. The fog you painted, the tokens you placed and the ink you drew stay exactly where they were, so you can stop a burning room on the frame you like and keep running the fight on it.

Honest limitsThe video file never leaves your computer. When you press Push to table your players get one still frame of it, with a line telling them the table's map is animated, because a phone on a hotel network does not want forty megabytes. A moving map also repaints the whole board every frame: on a laptop with lighting and a big fog mask this runs fine, and on a machine that struggles, Pause is one press away.

Push it to the table

Nothing on the board has reached anybody until you press this.

  1. Press Push to table and the map goes to every paired Satchel.
  2. Their phones get the map, the grid, the fog you revealed, and the tokens they are allowed to see.
  3. Press it again after a big change. A seat reading board out of sync is telling you something never landed.

Honest limitsSwitching to a different map detaches your table: they still hold the last board you pushed, and nothing new reaches them until you press it again, and the app says so at the moment it happens. If you have not pushed at all this session it tells you that too. For a television you need none of this: see the second screen, which needs no phones and no network.

A window for every screen

The board on the television, the fight at your elbow, the book in front of you.

  1. Press Screens on the header bar, or find it in the Everything menu.
  2. Open the ones you want, each in a window of its own: the party view, the muster and the fight, your book, or the clock and the calendar.
  3. Drag each window onto whichever monitor you want it on, and size it to that screen.
  4. Every window follows this Grimoire as you work, because they are views of one state rather than copies of it: what you change here changes there.
  5. Close any of them whenever you like. They come back where you left them the next time you open them.

The party window is the one to put on the television: it shows only what your players may see, the same half the second screen shows, so your ink and your hidden figures stay on your own screen.

Honest limitsThese are screens to read, not surfaces to drive: the controls inside a window are inert, so you run the fight from the main window and watch it on the monitor across the table. They are windows on this computer, so they want a second monitor plugged into it; a phone in somebody's hand is still Gnasher’s Satchel. And the party window plays a moving map by reading the same file from the same folder on this computer, so it cannot drift out of step with yours.

Open it on a starter map

The three starter maps exist so you can try all of this without hunting for a picture first. Open one, drop a few tokens, drag a wall across it and light a torch, and you will know in two minutes whether the board is the one you want.
