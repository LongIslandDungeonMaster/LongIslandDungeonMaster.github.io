A Running Series

VillainCraft

How the opposition actually operates when the party isn't looking. Not lore. Not location prep. Operations — loadouts, rotations, tells, escalation ladders, and what the villain does with the hours between your sessions.

Your villain has a calendar. Use it.

Most villain advice tells you who the villain is. Almost none of it tells you what he does on a Tuesday. That gap is where campaigns go flat: the big bad sits in his castle until the finale, and the players work out he's a stationary hit-point total with good dialogue.

These are my notes from thirty years behind the screen and five full runs of Curse of Strahd. They're about the machinery — what he prepared before he knocked, what he was really doing while they ate, and why the courteous version is scarier than the raging one. Everything here is free, complete, and written to be stolen.

The foundation

The Omnipresent Strahd

What made him frightening at my table was never the stat block. It was that he is the land, and the land does what he says — the fog, the roads, the weather, the coins. He doesn't have to be in the room to be in the scene.

Read this first. Everything else assumes it.
