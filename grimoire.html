Think of it like a binder

Right now your adventures live scattered — a Keeper in your downloads, a free pack in your inbox, last week's one-shot in a folder you'll never find at the table. Gnasher's Grimoire is the slipcase that holds every one of them. Open the binder and it's all there: every table searchable, every monster one keystroke away, every NPC ready to speak.

It's the same instinct as a good DM screen — everything you need in arm's reach — except the reach is three letters and a keypress, and the screen holds your entire library instead of four cardboard panels.

You're not buying a PDF you scroll.
You're buying a Keeper that drops into your Grimoire.

What it does at the table

The moves you reach for mid-session, when you need something now and can't afford to lose the room.

The Summon

A command palette over your whole library. Fuzzy-search tables, monsters, and NPCs across every book you own — hit Space, type, done. No tab-hunting, no flipping.

The Board

Float as many cards as you want and drag them where you like. Collapse a big d100 down to its title bar — it still rolls collapsed. Everything saves to the exact adventure you opened it in.

Bookmarks

Drop up to ten spots in a long adventure — auto-named from the scene you're on ("Battle Map — Gull's Tooth"). Click a chip and you're back there instantly.

The War Table

Muster a fight from your party and any Creature Keeper you own — browsed like chapters — then run full initiative, HP, and conditions without leaving the page. Monster attacks roll right there.

Install your own

Drag a downloaded Keeper onto the binder and it's in — its tables and monsters join the Summon, its art streams in when you open it. No account, no upload, no sign-in.

It also takes the files you already have: notes as .txt or .md, random tables as .csv or .tsv (they arrive rollable, with the die read off your own roll column), homebrew .json in the 5e‑tools shape (its monsters muster straight into the War Table), a saved web page, whole books as .epub, and .pdf for the Tome shelf. It ships with the free SRD and nothing else, so the homebrew is yours to bring — and everything you drop stays on your machine.

The Live Table

Read one code off the screen and your players link in with Gnasher's Satchel. Muster a fight and initiative lights up every screen; push handouts and whispers; their rolls come home to your log. No server, no account.

Yours, offline, forever

Runs 100% offline, like the Keepers themselves. Your whole binder exports to one file you can back up or move. No subscription to open what you already own.

New · Run the fight in place

The War Table

The hardest part of a combat is never the dice — it's keeping the whole board in your head while the table watches you stall. The War Table runs it for you, right on top of the adventure.

  • Muster from your party — split it, bring two of the six — and pull monsters from any Creature Keeper you own, grouped like chapters, with a quantity stepper ("3 orcs" auto-numbers them).
  • Initiative & rounds tracked, one button to advance the turn.
  • HP per combatant — type the damage, watch them drop.
  • Conditions as removable chips, and monster attacks that roll to-hit and damage straight into your log.

New · Connect the whole table

The live table

You run the night from the Grimoire. Your players carry Gnasher's Satchel, the free character app, on their phones, tablets, or laptops. Pair once and the two halves lock together, with no server and no account.

  • One code opens the table. Read it aloud, players type it in, and everyone is linked in seconds. No venue Wi-Fi? Same-room mode or a phone hotspot works too.
  • Initiative drives every screen. When you muster a fight and advance turns on the War Table, each player's screen lights up on their turn. You do nothing extra.
  • Push handouts and whispers. Send a map or a note to the whole table, or slip one player a private line.
  • Their rolls come home. Player rolls land in your dice log and the session Chronicle, labeled by name.
  • Phones sleep, seats are held. A dozing phone rejoins by itself and catches up on anything it missed.
